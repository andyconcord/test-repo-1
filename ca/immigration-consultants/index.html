What do you need to do next?

Most people who call us are already living and working in Canada. They usually need one of these three things.

Extend or change my work permit

Your work permit, graduate permit or study permit has an end date. We work out your best next permit and the safest timing, so you can keep living and working here while you wait.

Get permanent residence

Express Entry, a provincial nomination, or a route through your Canadian work experience. We check which ones you can use today, and what you need to do to get there.

Bring my partner or family

If you hold or are applying for a work permit or PR, your partner and children can often come with you, and your partner may be able to work. We make sure it is done right the first time.

Need more than one of these? Many people do. We plan them together in one consultation.

Not every "immigration consultant" is licensed. Here is how to tell.

In Canada, a real immigration consultant is licensed by the College of Immigration and Citizenship Consultants, the CICC, and has a licence number you can look up. Licensed consultants are the immigration specialists people use to extend a work permit, get permanent residence, or bring their family here. Anyone without a licence is not allowed to help with your application, and if something goes wrong, you carry the risk. Our Canadian team are licensed consultants. Their numbers are on this page.

Most visa problems we see do not come from a hard case. They come from small things: applying after the permit expired, picking a permit that does not lead to PR, a partner left off an application, advice copied from a forum that was true two years ago. By the time someone notices, the money is spent and the right to work is gone.

The good news: you can get a clear answer, from a licensed consultant, in 20 minutes.

Choosing an adviser

Four things to check before you pay anyone, including us

A licence you can look up: Every licensed consultant has an RCIC number you can check on the CICC public register. Ours are on this page. If someone will not give you theirs, walk away.

A price in writing, before you start: You should know the full cost before you pay a deposit. If the price is "it depends" after the first consultation, ask why.

Someone who knows today's rules: Canadian immigration rules change many times a year. Ask when the adviser last handled a case like yours.

Honesty about your chances: A good adviser will tell you when the answer is no, or not yet. We do, in the consultation, before you spend anything on an application.
